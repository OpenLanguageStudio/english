# Terminology

Grammar has its own words, and English textbooks and teachers use them all the time. Each entry here gives one term in plain language and one example. Articles in the course link to this page the first time they use a term.

## Parts of speech

```{glossary}
Noun
  A word that names a person, place, thing or idea. *Example: a teacher, London, a cup, love.*

Pronoun
  A short word that stands in for a noun, so the noun need not be repeated. *Example: Anna is tired. She is going home.*

Personal pronoun
  A word for a person or thing already known, such as I, you, she or they. *Example: I, you, he, she, it, we, they.*

Verb
  A word for an action or a state, such as run, sleep or be. *Example: I live in Leeds.*

Adjective
  A word that describes a noun, saying what it is like. *Example: a big house, a cold day.*

Adverb
  A word that says how, when or where something happens. *Example: She speaks slowly.*

Preposition
  A small word in front of a noun that shows place, time or direction, such as in, on or at. *Example: at the station.*

Conjunction
  A word that joins words, phrases or clauses, such as and, but or because. *Example: tea and coffee.*

Question word
  A word that opens a question asking for information, such as who, what or where. *Example: Where do you live?*
```

## Articles

```{glossary}
Definite article
  The word the, used for something specific or already known. *Example: the station in my town.*

Indefinite article
  The word a or an, used for one thing that is new or not specific. *Example: a sandwich, an apple.*
```

## Nouns

```{glossary}
Singular
  The form of a word for one person or thing. *Example: one cup.*

Plural
  The form of a word for more than one person or thing, usually with -s at the end. *Example: two cups.*

Countable noun
  A noun that can be counted, with a singular and a plural form. *Example: one egg, three eggs.*

Uncountable noun
  A noun for something that is not counted, such as a liquid or a material, with no plural form. *Example: milk, water, bread.*
```

## Verbs

```{glossary}
Infinitive
  The basic form of a verb, as a dictionary lists it, often with to in front. *Example: to work, to be.*

Conjugation
  The set of forms a verb takes for different people and times. *Example: I work, she works.*

Auxiliary verb
  A helping verb that goes with a main verb to make questions, negatives or tenses, such as do, be or have. *Example: Do you like tea?*

Contraction
  A short form made by joining two words and leaving letters out, marked with an apostrophe. *Example: I am becomes I'm.*

Tense
  The form of a verb that shows when something happens. *Example: I work (present), I worked (past).*

Present tense
  The tense for what is true now, or happens regularly. *Example: She lives in York.*

Past tense
  The tense for what happened before now. *Example: We lived in York.*

Future tense
  The forms for what will happen later, often made with will. *Example: I will call you tomorrow.*

Imperative
  The form of a verb for an instruction or a request. *Example: Sit down, please.*
```

## Comparing

```{glossary}
Comparative
  The form of an adjective that compares two things, often with -er or more. *Example: bigger, more expensive.*

Superlative
  The form of an adjective that marks the top of a group, often with -est or most. *Example: the biggest, the most expensive.*
```

## Sentences

```{glossary}
Subject
  The person or thing that does the action of the verb, usually before the verb. *Example: Tom plays football.*

Object
  The person or thing a verb acts on, usually after the verb. *Example: I like coffee.*

Direct object
  The person or thing that the action lands on directly. *Example: She reads a book.*

Indirect object
  The person who receives the direct object, often after to. *Example: Give the book to Sam.*

Clause
  A group of words with its own subject and verb. *Example: because it is raining.*

Main clause
  A clause that can stand alone as a sentence. *Example: I stay at home because it is raining.*

Subordinate clause
  A clause that cannot stand alone and depends on a main clause, often after because, when or if. *Example: I stay at home because it is raining.*

Relative clause
  A clause that describes a noun and starts with who, which or that. *Example: the man who lives next door.*

Word order
  The order the words of a sentence come in, which in English is usually the subject, then the verb, then the object. *Example: Anna drinks tea.*

Negation
  Making a sentence say "no" or "not", in English usually with not or don't. *Example: I don't like fish.*
```
