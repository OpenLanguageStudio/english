# Asking for information

A formal email asking for information goes to a school, an office or a company, and in English it follows a fixed frame. It opens with *Dear*, asks a few clear questions and closes with *Kind regards*.

The register here is formal, because the writer does not know the reader. A name is not always known, so *Dear Sir or Madam* stands in for it. The full forms *I am* and *I would* replace the short forms of a casual email. Each question is short and polite, and the phrase *I would like* makes a request softer than *I want*.

## Example: Writing to a language school

Paul Wagner lives in Hamburg. He wants to learn English in Brighton in the summer, so he writes to a language school.

> **Subject:** English course in July
>
> Dear Sir or Madam,
>
> My name is Paul Wagner and I am from Hamburg in Germany. I would like to take an English course at your school in July. I am a beginner.
>
> Could you please send me some information? How many hours are the lessons every day? How much does the course cost? Is there a room with a family near the school?
>
> Thank you very much.
>
> Kind regards,
> Paul Wagner

The first paragraph says who is writing and why. The second asks three direct questions, one per line of thought. *Could you please* is the polite way to ask for something. The full name goes under *Kind regards*.

## Building blocks

The formal frame, ready to reuse for any request:

| Phrase | Notes |
|---|---|
| {stack}`Dear Sir or Madam, \| Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren,` | when the name is not known |
| {stack}`Dear Ms Smith, \| Sehr geehrte Frau Smith,` | when the name is known |
| {stack}`My name is … \| Mein Name ist …` | first line: who is writing |
| {stack}`I would like to … \| Ich möchte …` | polite way to say what is wanted |
| {stack}`Could you please …? \| Könnten Sie bitte …?` | polite request |
| {stack}`How much does … cost? \| Wie viel kostet …?` | asking about a price |
| {stack}`Thank you very much. \| Vielen Dank.` | before the goodbye |
| {stack}`Kind regards, \| Mit freundlichen Grüßen,` | the usual formal goodbye |
| {stack}`Yours faithfully, \| Hochachtungsvoll,` | very formal, after *Dear Sir or Madam* |

## Summary

:::{admonition} Key points
:class: note

- Open with *Dear Sir or Madam* or *Dear Ms/Mr* and the surname.
- Say who is writing and why in the first paragraph.
- Ask short, polite questions with *Could you please* and *I would like*.
- Use full forms: *I am*, *I would*.
- Close with *Kind regards* and the full name.
:::

:::{admonition} Common mistakes
:class: caution

- *Hi* in a formal email: too casual; use **Dear Sir or Madam**.
- *\*Dear Mrs Paul,* → **Dear Mr Wagner,** (the title goes with the surname)
- *\*I want information.* → **Could you please send me some information?** (*I want* sounds rude here)
- *\*How much costs the course?* → **How much does the course cost?** (questions with *how much* need *does*)
:::
