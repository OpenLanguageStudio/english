# In the kitchen

Kitchen words in English come up every day: laying the table, finding the milk, asking for a spoon. This pack furnishes the room and sets the table.

The words fall into three groups: the furniture, the table setting and the things used for cooking. *Cook* is the one {term}`verb` in the pack. Every {term}`noun` here takes *a* in the singular and adds *-s* in the {term}`plural`, except *knife*, which becomes *knives*. The nouns go well with place phrases such as *on the table* and *in the fridge*.

## The words

| Word | Notes |
|---|---|
| {stack}`a table \| der Tisch` | plural *tables* |
| {stack}`a chair \| der Stuhl` | plural *chairs* |
| {stack}`a plate \| der Teller` | plural *plates* |
| {stack}`a cup \| die Tasse` | plural *cups*; for tea or coffee |
| {stack}`a glass \| das Glas` | plural *glasses* |
| {stack}`a knife \| das Messer` | plural *knives*; the *k* is silent |
| {stack}`a fork \| die Gabel` | plural *forks* |
| {stack}`a spoon \| der Löffel` | plural *spoons* |
| {stack}`a pan \| der Topf, die Pfanne` | plural *pans*; also *a saucepan* |
| {stack}`a fridge \| der Kühlschrank` | plural *fridges* |
| {stack}`a cooker \| der Herd` | plural *cookers*; American English *a stove* |
| {stack}`to cook \| kochen` | *she cooks* |

## Words that change in the plural

Most plurals just add *-s*: *a cup, two cups*. *Glass* adds *-es*, because the word already ends in *-s*: *glasses*. *Knife* changes its ending: *one knife, two knives*. The *k* at the start of *knife* is never spoken.

## Summary

The pack covers the furniture (*table*, *chair*, *fridge*, *cooker*), the table setting (*plate*, *cup*, *glass*, *knife*, *fork*, *spoon*) and the cooking pair *pan* and *cook*. Place phrases such as *on the table* and *in the fridge* put each thing where it belongs.
