# Clothes and colours

Clothes and colours in English go together in nearly every sentence about them: *a blue shirt*, *black shoes*. This pack names the everyday clothes and the colours that describe them.

A colour is an {term}`adjective`, and in English it always stands before the {term}`noun` it describes: *a red dress*, never *a dress red*. It also never changes its ending, so *red* is the same for one shirt and for ten. Some clothes come in pairs and are always {term}`plural`: *trousers*, *jeans* and *shoes* take *are*, not *is*.

## The words

## Trousers and jeans are plural

*Trousers* and *jeans* name one piece of clothing, but they are plural words: *my jeans are new*. A learner never says *a trouser*. To count them, English uses *a pair of*: *a pair of trousers*, *two pairs of jeans*. *Pants* means trousers in American English, but underwear in British English.
