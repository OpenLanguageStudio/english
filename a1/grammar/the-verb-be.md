# The verb *be*

The {term}`verb` *be* is the most common verb in English, and it has three forms in the present: *am*, *is* and *are*. It carries names, jobs, ages, feelings and places.

The form depends on the {term}`subject`, the person or thing the sentence is about. *I* takes *am*, *he*, *she* and *it* take *is*, and *you*, *we* and *they* take *are*. Speech nearly always uses the short form, the {term}`contraction`: *I'm*, *she's*, *we're*. For a question, *be* moves in front of the subject, and for a negative, *not* follows it. So *be* never needs the helper *do*.

## The forms: *am*, *is*, *are*

Every {term}`personal pronoun` has one form of *be*. The short forms join the pronoun and the verb with an apostrophe.

| | Full form | Short form |
|---|---|---|
| I | I am | I'm |
| he, she, it | she is | she's |
| you, we, they | we are | we're |

A name or a {term}`noun` takes the same form as its pronoun: *Tom is* like *he is*, *my parents are* like *they are*.

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
I {verb}`am` Anna.
: I am Anna.

She {verb}`is` from Leeds.
: She is from Leeds.

They {verb}`are` my friends.
: They are my friends.
:::

## Questions and short answers

A question swaps the subject and the verb: *You are tired* becomes *Are you tired?* The answer is often a short answer, with the pronoun and *be* alone.

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
{verb}`Are` you a student? Yes, I {verb}`am`.
: Are you a student? Yes, I am (a student).

{verb}`Is` he at home? No, he {verb}`isn't`.
: Is he at home? No, he is not (at home).

Where {verb}`is` the station?
: Where is the station (in this town)?
:::

:::{note}
A short answer with *yes* always uses the full form: *Yes, I am*, never *Yes, I'm*. A short answer with *no* uses the short form: *No, I'm not* or *No, it isn't*.
:::

## Negatives with *not*

{term}`Negation <negation>` puts *not* straight after *be*. Speech shortens it in one of two ways: *she isn't* or *she's not*. Both are correct. *I am not* has only one short form, *I'm not*.

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
I am {verb}`not` hungry.
: I am not hungry.

It is {verb}`not` far.
: It is not far.

We {verb}`aren't` ready.
: We are not ready.
:::

## When English uses *be*

*Be* says who someone is, what they do, where something is and how someone feels. English also uses *be* for age, and for being hungry, thirsty, cold or right. Many languages use *have* for these, but English uses *be*.

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
She {verb}`is` a nurse.
: She is a nurse.

I {verb}`am` twenty-five.
: I am twenty-five years old.

We {verb}`are` cold.
: We feel cold.
:::

:::{admonition} Common mistakes
:class: caution

- *\*She a teacher.* → She **is** a teacher. (English never leaves out *be*)
- *\*I have twenty years.* → I **am** twenty. (age uses *be*)
- *\*Do you are tired?* → **Are** you tired? (*be* makes its own questions)
- *\*I'm hungry? Yes, I'm.* → Yes, I **am**. (no short form at the end)
:::

## Summary

:::{admonition} Key points
:class: note

- Three present forms: *I am*, *he/she/it is*, *you/we/they are*.
- Speech uses the short forms: *I'm*, *she's*, *we're*, *isn't*, *aren't*.
- Questions swap the subject and the verb: *Are you tired?*
- Negatives add *not*: *I'm not*, *it isn't*.
- Age, hunger and cold use *be*: *I'm twenty*, *I'm hungry*.
:::
