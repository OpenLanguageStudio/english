# Ordering food and drink

Ordering food and drink in English runs on two polite openers: *Can I have …?* and *I'd like …*. With *please* and *thank you*, they cover a café, a pub and a restaurant from the first order to the bill.

A café visit follows a short, predictable script. The staff ask *What can I get you?*, the order follows, and the bill or the payment closes it. *I'd like* is the short form of *I would like*, a softer way of saying *I want*. *I want* sounds rude when ordering, so English avoids it here. A drink or a dish takes *a*, *an* or a number in front: *a coffee*, *two teas*.

## When to use it

- Ordering at the counter of a café or a pub
- Ordering at the table in a restaurant
- Asking for the menu, for water or for the bill
- Paying by card or in cash

## Key phrases

### Ordering

| Phrase | Notes |
|---|---|
| {stack}`Can I have a coffee, please? \| I would like a coffee, please.` | the most common order |
| {stack}`I'd like a cheese sandwich, please. \| I would like a cheese sandwich, please.` | a little more formal |
| {stack}`A tea, please. \| I would like a tea, please.` | short and fine at a counter |
| {stack}`Could I see the menu, please? \| Please give me the menu.` | |

### Questions from the staff

| Phrase | Notes |
|---|---|
| {stack}`What can I get you? \| What would you like?` | the usual opening |
| {stack}`Anything else? \| Would you like anything more?` | *No, that's all, thanks.* |
| {stack}`Eat in or take away? \| Do you want to eat here or take it with you?` | American English: *for here or to go* |

### Paying

| Phrase | Notes |
|---|---|
| {stack}`Can I have the bill, please? \| I would like to pay now, please.` | American English: *the check* |
| {stack}`Can I pay by card? \| Can I pay by card?` | |
| {stack}`How much is it? \| What is the price?` | |

## Answering the staff

The staff's questions need only short answers. *Yes, please* and *No, thanks* do most of the work, and a size or a choice can stand alone.

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Anything else? No, that's all, thanks.
: Would you like anything more? No, I do not want anything more, thank you.

Small or large? Large, please.
: Do you want a small one or a large one? A large one, please.

Eat in or take away? Take away, please.
: Do you want to eat here or take it with you? I want to take it with me, please.
:::

## Culture note

In a British pub, nobody comes to the table: drinks are ordered and paid for at the bar. Many cafés work the same way. In a restaurant, a tip of about ten percent is usual unless the bill already includes a service charge. *Cheers* means thank you as well as a toast.

## Summary

:::{admonition} Key points
:class: note

- Order with *Can I have …, please?* or *I'd like …, please.*
- A drink or a dish takes *a*, *an* or a number: *a coffee*, *two teas*.
- Answer the staff briefly: *Yes, please*, *No, thanks*, *Large, please*.
- Close with *Can I have the bill, please?*
:::

:::{admonition} Common mistakes
:class: caution

- *\*I want a coffee.* → **Can I have** a coffee, please? (*I want* sounds rude)
- *\*Can I have coffee?* at a counter → Can I have **a** coffee? (one cup takes *a*)
- *\*Can I have the bill?* with no *please* → Can I have the bill, **please**? (*please* is expected)
:::
